## Fixture-Lifetime Control Authority

The fixture has exactly one logical authoritative provenance source. This section defines its facts and
behavior, not whether they are represented by a file, database, service, or another storage technology.
The owner is accountable for the authority through final teardown and is the sole activation authority;
an authorized operator records the facts produced by that operation, and the reviewer named by the
applicable gate attests the evidence required before the owner may activate a candidate target binding,
baseline manifest, reconciliation, or teardown result.

Each immutable revision has the following closed logical content. A concrete representation may split
the content across records only when one active revision digest covers the complete snapshot and all
append-only history remains reachable.

| Field | Type and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| `schemaVersion` | Integer, exactly `2`; version 2 adds first-manual ratification and managed-risk facts without changing deployment identity, target binding, baseline, fixed-state, or D-018 semantics. |
| `fixtureIdentity` | Exact dedicated subscription, `example-test-rg`, `example-test-sql`, and `ProphetsWay.Example` identities; immutable after first activation. |
| `revisionNumber` | Positive integer exactly one greater than the preceding active revision. |
| `previousRevisionDigest` | SHA-256 of the preceding active revision; absent only on the first revision. |
| `revisionDigest` | SHA-256 of the complete canonical revision, recorded independently by the activating review. |
| `deploymentIdentityLedger` | Append-only entry for every atomically reserved deployment identity set: two-digit root sequence and exact root name, UTC allocation time, exact module prefix, all four direct authored module names, all five expected deployment resource IDs, preview attempt key and input digest, deployment attempt key when allocated, lifecycle state, and terminal evidence. The first root sequence is `01`; later reservations increase monotonically through `99`. An entry is never deleted or made reusable. Prefixes are unique for the complete fixture lifetime. |

neither this boundary nor deletion authority. The authority and its complete history must remain readable
at least through successful final-teardown verification.

## Repair Design Choices

| Choice | Rejected alternative | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reviewed target-binding record with an independently recorded digest | Let endpoint variables and the connection string corroborate each other | Those values share one process-input trust source and can consistently mislabel a non-Azure target. |
| One owner-controlled, storage-neutral fixture-lifetime authority with append-only revisions | Treat 30-day run directories, Azure state, or whichever manifest is nearby as the source of truth | Deployment-identity burns, active target and baseline identities, and unresolved survivors outlive individual runs; disagreement must be reconciled rather than guessed. |
| Strictly non-mutating authority-outage behavior | Permit emergency recovery, cleanup, or teardown merely to reduce continuing exposure | A second consequential authority path would contradict the sole-authority design; restoration or reconciliation, fresh durable preallocation, and separate owner approval are required before mutation. |
| One concrete, permanently consumed first-manual certification-attempt identity | Treat the scope label, a wave run ID, or the human principal as the attempt discriminator | D-022's exception must be replay-resistant across multiple waves and operation attempts; one authority-bound identity makes the aggregate observable without merging its per-operation approvals. |
| Protected just-in-time exact address with a zero-through-five-minute inclusive predicate | Store the literal address in source or evidence, call an unbounded age "immediate," or widen the rule | D-028 makes the last accepted and first stale observations objectively decidable while preserving one-address reachability and protected timestamps. |
| Owner-ratified managed residual risk | Label an operational reserve as a finite cap or retain D-017's impossible first-manual eligibility test | D-026 permits one explicit unbounded-tail acceptance after independent review and exact ratification; it does not change D-018 or make billing cessation finite. |
| Immutable USD 5.00 threshold with terminal create/wave exhaustion | Permit reapproval to raise the threshold or restart disposable work under the same ratification | Billing can continue while D-018 prohibits mutation, so USD 5.00 stops and escalates without becoming a cap; D-028 permanently exhausts only further creates and waves while preserving separately authorized remediation and teardown. |
| Immutable per-wave accepted-exposure upper bound 128, with later active limits only in `1..128` | Make exposure coverage equal each active later limit, allow an unbounded positive replacement value, or treat coverage as wave authority | D-029 preserves one immutable ratification exposure statement while allowing a lower later-wave limit after fresh derivation and separate approvals; a value above the reviewed bound needs a newly reviewed ratification rather than silently expanding accepted risk. |
| Full-object `EFTools.LifecyclePolicy.JCS.v1` canonical artifact and digest | Hash original file bytes, hash selected fields, inherit the ratification profile implicitly, or accept semantically equivalent alternate encodings | The gate-bearing policy needs one independently reproducible scope and byte sequence across every producer; exact schema-first validation, RFC 8785, field-specific NFC/ASCII, canonical UTC/USD strings, and source-byte equality make every mismatch decidable. |
| One sealed caller-supplied Microsoft Entra administrator metadata object, one root-owned closed projection to the confirmed SQL-server AVM, and one required client-address scalar | Authentication and network branch matrices or a caller-supplied principal-type discriminator | The caller supplies only `mode`, `administratorLogin`, `administratorObjectId`, and `tenantId`; root adds the settled `azureADOnlyAuthentication: true` and `principalType: 'User'` literals while mapping login, SID, and tenant ID. This fixes deployment administration without selecting a runtime identity or grant. |
| Exact typed table-content equality | Row count alone or a mandated hashing technology | Cardinality cannot detect in-place edits, while the requirement can state exact equality and let implementation choose a representation that satisfies falsification cases. |
| Distinct preview and deployment attempts under one atomically burned root-sequence/module-prefix identity set, with exact input-digest identity | Reuse any member after failure, allocate a different prefix for deployment, or deploy changed inputs | One atomic burn preserves all five direct authored deployment identities, while the shared immutable digest proves deployment uses exactly what the successful preview examined. |
| Direct composition of the four D-020 pinned AVM modules, producing four direct module deployment records in addition to the root record | Force all contained resources through one wrapper deployment, use unversioned modules, or use raw resources | The four direct declarations preserve the settled module boundaries and require distinct names at their actual scopes. AVM-internal implementation deployments remain distinguishable from the five direct authored records and do not alter the reviewed identity set. |
| No `.bicepparam` in this authoring cycle, with an exact repository validation diagnostic | Count expected missing-required diagnostics as a successful `build-params` run | Required approval-bound parameters make an intentionally incomplete file's native process result ambiguous; absence is explicitly permitted and produces one coherent pass state. |
| Settled ProphetsWay-wide resource grammar plus D-021's separate root-sequence and externally allocated module-prefix grammar | Retain one shared module-deployment name, generate a timestamp inside Bicep, or silently add a fallback suffix | The owner settled `example-test-rg`, `example-test-sql`, `eastus`, exact root and direct module deployment-name forms, and exact `ProphetsWay.Example`; a duplicate prefix, exhausted root sequence, or resource-name conflict stops the applicable allocation or attempt because no fallback is approved. |
| Fixed-data, wave-owned inspection, and catalog inspection as distinct access purposes | Treat every name passed to `ConfigureExisting` as equivalent | The fixed database is never disposable, while inherited Gate 1 guards must inspect current-wave disposable stores and the catalog without gaining foreign-database access. |
| Cumulative-attempt unknown-cleanup reserve plus distinct approved-hold and involuntary-survivor states | Reserve unknown cleanup only for concurrently present survivors or call every physical survivor retained | Sequential survivors can occur after earlier cleanup resolves and creation resumes, so every one of the $C$ possible attempts needs a finite cessation-backed reserve; physical survival creates neither approval nor deletion authority. |
| Strict textual endpoint and database canonicalization | Treat DNS aliases, ports, prefixes, or database collation as equivalent | Network equivalence and SQL collation are not proof that the reviewed target was used. |
| Explicit `Uninitialized` plus cause-sensitive `Clean`, `Contaminated`, `Unknown`, and terminal `Removed` with closed operation kinds | Let initialization enter from an implicit state, let a probe replace the retained cause, or require teardown to recreate `Clean` content | Initialization, successful-wave restoration, failed-operation recovery, and teardown each have distinct authority; probes preserve cause, teardown proves absence, and terminal removal cannot re-enter the lifecycle. |

### Resource Naming Contract

`{context}-{env}-{subContext?}-{type}-{subType?}-{subSubType?}` under the resource's Azure naming rules.
`context`, `env`, and `type` are meaningful required segments. Optional segments are omitted rather than
filled with generic or repeated tokens when they add no meaning. A child or attached resource name must
include its parent attachment path before the segments that identify the child. A redundant
`ProphetsWay` segment or tag is forbidden.

never-reused root attempt identity preserved from `D-004` and refined by `D-021`. The module prefix is

letter of `context=example`, `t` is the first lowercase letter of `environment=test`, no subcontext is
present, and `MMddHHmm` comes from the UTC allocation time. It is not an optional resource segment,
random value, fallback suffix, or Bicep-generated timestamp.

### Cost Semantics And Managed-Risk Stop State

D-017 remains unchanged as the default and outside this exact ratified scope. Its conservative formula is
$M = F + N + (C \times O) + (R \times H) + (C \times U)$ with $R=1$ and $C$, never $K$, multiplying
unknown-cleanup exposure. Missing finite cessation evidence makes $M$ `Unbounded` and ordinarily ineligible.

For the ratified first-manual scope, `Unbounded` is accepted rather than converted into a false finite
number. USD 3.1127 is exactly `ConditionalNormalEstimateNotCap`. USD 29.86 and USD 184.42 are each exactly
`IllustrativeNonCap`. They remain visible for comparison, but none limits the invoice, selects a threshold,
or grants authority. Immutable USD 5.00 is the operating stop trigger, not a cost cap. Current cost evidence
is recalculated from pricing no more than 24 hours old at every applicable approval, including explicit
subscription offer and backup-cost treatment.

`Eligible` requires the exact ratification, aggregate certification-attempt identity, wave-admission basis,
immutable `coveredCumulativeAttempts=128`, an active `maxCumulativeDatabases` satisfying the D-029 branch,
and one byte-identity-verified `EFTools.LifecyclePolicy.JCS.v1` lifecycle-policy digest; all current protected
inputs and separate approval; a valid reconciled
authority; pricing age no greater than 24 hours; a completed daily review before its due time; complete cost
and operation evidence; accrued or conservatively estimated spend strictly below USD 5.00; no unmodeled
non-zero charge; no unresolved cleanup; every lifecycle limit intact; and `CreateWaveOpen`. The first failed
or unknown cleanup, missed review due time, stale pricing, unmodeled non-zero charge, authority outage or
`Unreconciled` state, limit breach, or incomplete evidence sets `Stopped` immediately. Spend greater than or
equal to USD 5.00 sets `Stopped`, irreversibly sets `CreateWavePermanentlyExhausted`, and changes the aggregate
to `RemediationOnly`.

`Stopped` refuses new creates and later waves. The current attempt follows its existing failure disposition;
no forced mutation is inferred. For a non-threshold trigger, return to `Eligible` requires complete
reconciliation, current pricing, a timely review, resolution of the trigger and every survivor, acknowledged
incidents, complete evidence, a fresh attempt, and fresh owner reapproval. Threshold exhaustion has no such
return: no owner reapproval, lower later observation, accounting correction, new pricing, or threshold
increase can restore create/wave admission under this ratification. Exact survivor inspection, cleanup, and
final teardown remain separately possible only through their valid-authority, fresh-attempt, current-pricing,
owner-acknowledgement, separate-approval, and operation-specific gates; they never restore
`CreateWaveOpen`. During a D-018 outage, incident evidence is retained for later reconciliation and only
non-mutating inventory, evidence capture, and reconciliation proceed. Billing may continue beyond USD 5.00
and every illustrative amount. No stop, reconciliation, reapproval, acknowledgement, inspection result, or
cleanup result grants deletion authority or changes D-018.

### Incident Escalation Contract

Every `SYS-29` trigger creates exactly one incident identity in the same logical stop transition. The
escalation actor is the current operation-attempt issuer when an operation detects the trigger, the monitoring
owner acting in that role when a daily review detects it, or the reconciliation operator when reconciliation
first discovers an interrupted or missing escalation. The recipient is always the human owner in the
`monitoringOwner` role through the owner-approved `incidentEscalationRouteId`. Even when actor and recipient
are the same human, creation and acknowledgement are distinct recorded role actions; creating the incident
does not acknowledge it.

The closed escalation state is `DispatchPending`, `DeliveredAwaitingAcknowledgement`, `DeliveryFailed`,
`AcknowledgementOverdue`, or `Acknowledged`. The stop revision first records `DispatchPending`. After that
revision is durable and before the detecting path returns its terminal result or evaluates any later gate,
the actor must attempt delivery and durably record either `DeliveredAwaitingAcknowledgement` with immutable
delivery evidence or `DeliveryFailed` with the observed failure category. An interrupted path left at
`DispatchPending` is treated as failed delivery when next reconciled. During authority outage, D-018 permits
only non-mutating incident evidence and delivery attempts bound to the last known revision; no competing
active revision is created, and the evidence is appended only after reconciliation.

The owner acknowledgement must bind the incident ID, stop-revision digest, trigger, known/unknown resource
set, USD 5.00 threshold, accrued-cost evidence, current pricing age, continuing-cost status, and next
permitted action. It must be durably recorded no later than the earlier of the next daily-review due time and
any request for inspection, cleanup, recovery, teardown, or non-threshold resumption approval. Passing that
due time without acknowledgement records `AcknowledgementOverdue`. Failed delivery, interrupted dispatch,
or absent/late acknowledgement keeps managed-risk state `Stopped`, blocks every new approval and all creates
and waves, and supplies no mutation authority. The owner may recover the escalation only by directly
reviewing the retained incident, recording `Acknowledged`, then separately approving a fresh operation under
current pricing and valid reconciled authority. `Acknowledged` proves notice only; it is not operation
approval, cleanup authority, deletion authority, reconciliation, or threshold resumption.

Every incident record contains aggregate certification-attempt ID, incident ID, trigger, detecting actor and
role, recipient role, route reference, stop revision, `openedAtUtc`, `dispatchAttemptedAtUtc`, delivery state
and evidence or failure category, acknowledgement due time, `acknowledgedAtUtc` and acknowledgement evidence
when reached, known and unknown resources, pricing age, threshold and accrued cost, create/wave disposition,
continuing-cost status, and next permitted action. Exact timestamps and protected route destination remain
protected; routine evidence carries opaque references and closed results only. Escalation evidence is
append-only and remains readable through final teardown.
